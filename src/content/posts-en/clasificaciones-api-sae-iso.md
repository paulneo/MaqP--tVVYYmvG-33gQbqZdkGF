When you buy oil, you see codes like **15W-40 CK-4 API SN Plus**. Each
part of that label means something specific. This article teaches you
how to read the three main classifications: **API** (oil quality),
**SAE** (viscosity), and **ISO** (viscosity for hydraulic oils).

## API (American Petroleum Institute)

**API** defines **quality levels and compatibility** of oil with
different engines.

API oils split into two groups by engine type:

### S Group: gasoline engines

**S** = Service or Spark ignition.

Codes: SN, SP, SQ (the most modern).

- **SN** (2010): gasoline engines from 2011+.
- **SP** (2020): gasoline engines from 2020+ with LSPI (Low-Speed
  Pre-Ignition prevention) technology.
- **SQ** (2024+): latest generation.

The letters progress through the alphabet — each new category is
backward-compatible with earlier ones.

### C Group: diesel engines

**C** = Commercial or Compression ignition.

Codes: CJ-4, CK-4, FA-4.

- **CI-4** (2002): pre-2007 diesel engines with EGR but no DPF. High
  ZDDP (1,200 ppm).
- **CJ-4** (2007): engines with **DPF and SCR**. ZDDP reduced to
  1,200 ppm max. Optimized detergent-dispersants.
- **CK-4** (2017): an improvement over CJ-4. Optimized for extended
  drain. Better shear stability.
- **FA-4** (2017): lower viscosities (10W-30, 15W-30) for fuel
  efficiency. **Compatible with specific 2017+ engines**.

### How to choose the right API category

**General rule**: use the **most modern** category compatible with
your engine.

- **2007-2016 diesel engine** with DPF/SCR: use **CJ-4 or CK-4**.
- **2017+ diesel engine**: use **CK-4** or **FA-4** (if approved by the
  manual).
- **Pre-2007 diesel engine** (no DPF): can use **CI-4** or a more
  modern category (also works).

**NEVER** use an older category in a more modern engine. Example: using
**CI-4 in an engine with a DPF will clog the DPF** within a few
months.

## SAE (Society of Automotive Engineers)

**SAE** defines the oil's **viscosity** — how well it flows at
different temperatures.

### Monograde oils

A single number. Examples:

- **SAE 30**: medium viscosity, typical in moderate climates.
- **SAE 40**: thicker, engines with more blow-by.
- **SAE 50**: very thick, older worn engines.

Monogrades are used less and less — they don't perform well across
variable climates.

### Multigrade oils

Two numbers separated by "W" (winter). The most common is **15W-40**.

Format: `XW-Y`

- **X**: cold (winter) viscosity.
- **Y**: hot viscosity (at 100°C).

### The W number (cold)

Indicates **how well the oil flows in the cold** — specifically at
sub-zero temperatures.

| W Grade | Flows down to                 |
| ------- | ------------------------------ |
| 0W      | -40°C                          |
| 5W      | -35°C                          |
| 10W     | -30°C                          |
| 15W     | -25°C                          |
| 20W     | -15°C                          |
| 25W     | -10°C                          |

**Lower W number = better cold-weather performance**.

### The final number (hot)

Indicates **viscosity at 100°C** in centistokes.

| Grade | Viscosity at 100°C (cSt) | Typical use                    |
| ----- | ------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| 20    | 5.6-9.3                   | Modern light-duty cars          |
| 30    | 9.3-12.5                  | Cars and medium diesel engines  |
| 40    | 12.5-16.3                 | Heavy diesel engines             |
| 50    | 16.3-21.9                 | Very old engines or high load    |
| 60    | 21.9-26.1                 | Extreme applications             |

**Higher number = thicker when hot**.

### Common examples in diesel engines

- **15W-40**: the most common in temperate climates.
- **10W-40**: better starts in moderate cold.
- **5W-40 synthetic**: cold climates.
- **10W-30 synthetic**: modern fuel-efficient engines.
- **15W-30**: modern extended-drain (FA-4).

## Choosing the right viscosity

**Depends on**:

1. The **coldest ambient temperature** the engine will experience.
2. Normal **operating temperature**.
3. **Engine type and condition**.
4. The **manufacturer's specification**.

**Basic rule**:

- **Cold climate** (-25°C to 20°C): synthetic 5W-40 or 10W-40.
- **Temperate climate** (0°C to 35°C): mineral or semi-synthetic
  15W-40.
- **Hot climate** (20°C to 45°C): 15W-40 or 20W-50.

**Engine with heavy wear** (high blow-by): use a higher viscosity
(15W-50 or 20W-50) for better sealing.

**Very new engine** with tight tolerances: follow the manufacturer's
exact specification — don't experiment.

## ISO (International Organization for Standardization)

**ISO** classifies **HYDRAULIC** oils by viscosity. It's not used for
engine oils.

### Common ISO grades

| Grade  | Viscosity at 40°C (cSt) | Typical use                    |
| ------ | ------------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| ISO 22 | 22                        | Very cold climates                |
| ISO 32 | 32                        | Cold climates, low load           |
| **ISO 46** | **46**                | **STANDARD in heavy equipment**  |
| ISO 68 | 68                        | Hot climate or heavy loads         |
| ISO 100 | 100                      | Demanding applications             |
| ISO 150 | 150                      | Very high loads, low RPM           |

**The most common** in heavy construction equipment (excavators,
loaders, bulldozers): **ISO 46**.

### ISO H, HM, HV, HVI

Along with the numeric grade, ISO specifications add letters:

- **HL**: mineral hydraulic oil with oxidation and corrosion
  inhibitors. General use.
- **HM**: mineral oil with **anti-wear** additives. Heavy-duty
  equipment.
- **HV**: mineral oil with **viscosity index improvers**. Variable
  climates.
- **HVI**: high viscosity index — very stable against temperature
  changes.

**Typical example** on an excavator: **ISO 46 HM** or **ISO 46 HV**
hydraulic oil.

## How to read a complete label

**Typical example**: a commercial diesel engine oil.

```
CATERPILLAR DEO 15W-40 API CJ-4/CI-4 ACEA E9/E7
```

Deciphering it:

- **CATERPILLAR DEO**: brand and product.
- **15W-40**: SAE viscosity (15W cold, 40 hot).
- **API CJ-4/CI-4**: meets API category CJ-4 (engines with a DPF) and
  CI-4 (pre-2007 engines). Backward-compatible.
- **ACEA E9/E7**: meets European ACEA standards for heavy trucks.

Another example, for hydraulic oil:

```
MOBIL DTE 25 ISO 46 HM
```

- **MOBIL DTE 25**: brand and product.
- **ISO 46**: viscosity of 46 cSt at 40°C.
- **HM**: mineral oil with anti-wear additives.

## Other classifications worth knowing

### ACEA (Europe)

A European system similar to API but with different logic:

- **A/B**: gasoline and light-duty diesel engines.
- **C**: engines with aftertreatment (DPF, SCR).
- **E**: heavy diesel engines.

Examples: **ACEA E7** (equivalent to CI-4/CJ-4), **ACEA E9**
(equivalent to CJ-4/CK-4).

### JASO (Japan)

A Japanese system, mainly for motorcycles and specific applications.

- **JASO MA**: 4-stroke motorcycles with wet clutches.
- **JASO MB**: 4-stroke motorcycles with dry clutches.
- **JASO DH-1/DH-2**: Japanese diesel engines.

### Manufacturer specifications

Every major manufacturer has its own specifications:

- **Caterpillar**: ECF-3, ECF-2 (for its engines).
- **Cummins**: CES 20081, CES 20086.
- **Volvo**: VDS-3, VDS-4, VDS-4.5.
- **Mercedes**: MB 228.51, MB 228.52.
- **Deutz**: DQC III, DQC IV.

A good commercial oil usually meets several of these specifications at
once, all listed on its label.

## In summary

The three key classification systems:

**API**: quality and engine compatibility. S (gasoline) and C (diesel)
groups. Modern diesel engines require CJ-4 or CK-4.

**SAE**: oil viscosity. Multigrades like 15W-40 are the standard in
temperate-climate diesel engines. The W number indicates cold
performance; the final number indicates hot performance.

**ISO**: viscosity for hydraulic oils. ISO 46 is the standard in heavy
equipment. It's NOT used for engine oils.

**Golden rule**: always check the manufacturer's manual to know the
exact specification your engine requires. Never use older categories
than what the engine requires. When oils of different specifications
get mixed, the protection of the more modern one is lost.
