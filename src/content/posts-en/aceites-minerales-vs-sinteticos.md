Synthetic oil cleans internal deposits better than mineral oil that accumulated over years. Those deposits can dislodge and clog the filter or cause leaks through hardened seals. Recommendation for older engines: a gradual transition using semi-synthetic first, or wait for an overhaul before switching to full synthetic. Never switch in the last phase of an engine's service life."
  - q: "What's the difference between semi-synthetic and full synthetic?"
    a: "SEMI-SYNTHETIC (also called 'premium mineral' or 'blend'): a mix of mineral oil with 10-30% synthetic oil. It gives marginal performance improvements at an intermediate cost.   - q: "Is synthetic oil 'thinner' than mineral?"
    a: "In NOMINAL viscosity they're the same — a synthetic 15W-40 has the same viscosity as a mineral 15W-40 at the same reference temperature. What changes is the STABILITY of that viscosity: synthetic oil keeps its viscosity nearly constant across a wide temperature range (high viscosity index), while mineral oil loses more viscosity when hot and gains more when cold. **Mineral oils** dominated the market for nearly 100 years. **Synthetic
oils** came later with better performance but higher cost. Today,
**semi-synthetics** occupy the middle ground. This article compares
all three and explains when each is justified.

## The three types by composition

### 1. Mineral oil

**Origin**: refined petroleum.

**Typical composition**: 100% mineral base oil + additives.

**Cost**: the most economical.

**Service life**: the shortest.

**Application**: standard engines, light-to-medium use, regular
maintenance.

### 2. Semi-synthetic oil (blend)

**Composition**: 70-90% mineral + 10-30% synthetic + additives.

**Cost**: 40-80% more than mineral.

**Service life**: 30-50% more than mineral.

**Application**: middle ground for modern engines that don't require
full synthetic.

### 3. Full synthetic oil

**Origin**: chemical synthesis (PAO, esters, PAG).

**Composition**: 90-100% synthetic + additives.

**Cost**: 3-5x more than mineral.

**Service life**: 3-5x more than mineral.

**Application**: premium engines, extreme conditions, extended drain,
when specified by the manufacturer.

## Advantages and disadvantages compared

### Mineral oil

**Advantages**:
- Low cost.
- Widely available.
- Acceptable performance under standard conditions.
- Well understood technically.

**Disadvantages**:
- Limited temperature range.
- Short service life.
- Loses viscosity quickly with temperature.
- Higher consumption from evaporation.

### Synthetic oil

**Advantages**:
- **Excellent performance at extreme temperatures** (intense cold and
  high heat).
- **Long service life** (3-5x mineral).
- **Lower self-consumption** (evaporates less).
- **Lower fuel consumption** (2-4% in some engines).
- **Better protection** during cold starts.
- Superior **chemical stability**.
- **Less internal deposit formation**.

**Disadvantages**:
- **High cost** (3-5x mineral).
- **May be incompatible** with older seals (engines built before
  1990-1995).
- **In old engines** it can dislodge deposits and cause problems.
- **Overkill** for occasional light use.

### Semi-synthetic oil

**Advantages**:
- Better performance than pure mineral.
- Intermediate cost.
- Compatible with most modern engines.
- A good middle ground.

**Disadvantages**:
- Doesn't reach full-synthetic performance.
- Not always sufficient for very demanding applications.

## When to choose each

### Use mineral when:

- **Older engine** (pre-2000) with no specific restrictions.
- **Light, occasional use** (under 50,000 km/year).
- **Frequent changes** are already planned.
- **Tight budget**.
- **Engine history** shows synthetic was never used previously (don't
  switch near end of service life).

### Use semi-synthetic when:

- **Modern engine** (post-2000), standard use.
- **Medium use** (50,000-100,000 km/year).
- **Cost-benefit sweet spot**.
- **Moderate extended drain** (15,000-25,000 km between changes).

### Use full synthetic when:

- **The manufacturer specifies it**: mandatory.
- **High-end engine**: modern engines with SCR and DPF usually require
  it.
- **Extreme conditions**: very cold or very hot climates, continuous
  heavy operation.
- **Extended drain**: changes every 30,000-50,000 km (with SOS
  analysis).
- **High utilization**: commercial engines that rack up a lot of
  mileage.
- **Critical applications**: where engine failure would be
  catastrophic.

## Modern engines usually require synthetic

Modern engines (post-2010) tend to **REQUIRE** synthetic or
semi-synthetic oil because of:

- **Tighter internal engine tolerances**.
- **Higher operating temperatures** (closer to the fatigue point of
  mineral oils).
- **DPF sensitive to ash** (low-quality mineral oils will clog the
  DPF).
- **SCR** requires oils with low deposit formation.
- **VGT turbos** with tolerances that demand stable oil.

Always check the manufacturer's manual — many post-2010 engines
specifically **disqualify** standard mineral oil.

## Switching from mineral to synthetic in an older engine

**Typical scenario**: you have an engine with 400,000-800,000 km
running fine on mineral oil. Is it worth switching to synthetic?

**Considerations**:

1. **Engine with heavy internal deposits**: synthetic cleans
   aggressively. It can dislodge deposits and clog filters or cause
   leaks through hardened seals.

2. **Seal leak risk**: modern synthetic oils are compatible with most
   seals, but very old engines (pre-1990) may have seals that can be
   damaged.

3. **Cost**: if the engine is already close to an overhaul, it's not
   worth investing in expensive synthetic.

**Recommendations**:

- **Engine with 400,000-600,000 km**: SAFE transition using
  semi-synthetic first.
- **Engine with 600,000-800,000 km**: run an SOS analysis on the
  current oil. If it's OK, transition gradually.
- **Engine with 800,000+ km**: probably not worth it. Wait for the
  overhaul.

## Extended drain

The practice of **spacing out oil changes** to reduce cost and
downtime.

**With mineral oil**: extended drain limited to 15,000-20,000 km.

**With semi-synthetic**: up to 25,000-30,000 km.

**With premium full synthetic**: up to 40,000-60,000 km.

**Requirement**: **periodic SOS analysis** to confirm the oil is still
holding up. Without analysis, there's no way to know if the oil is
spent.

**Economic benefit**: even though synthetic oil costs 3-5x more per
liter, if it lasts 3-4x longer, the cost per km ends up similar or
lower. Plus savings on labor from less frequent changes.

## In summary

**Mineral oils** are the most economical and remain the main choice
for older engines and light use. **Semi-synthetics** are the ideal
middle ground for standard modern engines. **Full synthetics** are the
premium choice — higher cost but longer service life and better
performance.

**Cost per liter**: mineral USD 4-8, semi-synthetic USD 8-14,
synthetic USD 15-30 (2026 references).

**General recommendation**: always follow the engine manufacturer's
specification. Modern engines (post-2010) usually require synthetic or
semi-synthetic. Older engines accept mineral without issues.

Under intensive operation (more than 100,000 km/year), synthetic pays
for itself through extended service life and lower consumption. Under
light operation, mineral oil covers the need amply.
